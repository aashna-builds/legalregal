# Internal Policy Review Checklist

Reference detail for the policy-review skill. General practice for pharma / medical device / aesthetics companies — not legal advice.

## Structural elements every policy should have

- **Purpose & scope** — what the policy covers and who it applies to (all employees? specific roles/markets/entities?)
- **Definitions** — key terms defined, especially regulatory or technical terms used elsewhere in the document
- **Roles & responsibilities** — who owns the policy, who enforces it, who employees escalate to
- **The actual requirements/procedure** — specific and measurable, not just aspirational language
- **Enforcement & consequences** — what happens on a violation
- **Exceptions process** — how a deviation gets approved, if ever
- **Version control** — effective date, version number, named owner, next scheduled review date

A policy missing several of these, especially version control and named ownership, is a real gap to flag regardless of type.

## By policy type

**HR / employment policies** — check for: alignment with current employment law in the relevant jurisdiction(s), a clear complaint/escalation path, non-retaliation language, and an acknowledgment/attestation mechanism (how employees confirm they've read it).

**Data privacy policies** — check for: which privacy regime(s) it's meant to comply with (e.g., GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, or a health-data-specific regime if patient data is involved given Hugel's medical aesthetics business), data subject rights described, breach notification procedure and timeline, and whether it covers data shared with distributors/HCP partners, not just internal data.

**Quality / regulatory SOPs** — check for: current FDA (or applicable local regulator) citation, alignment with QMS/ISO framework if referenced, deviation/CAPA (corrective and preventive action) process, and adverse event / complaint handling procedure with clear timelines.

**Code of conduct / ethics** — check for: anti-bribery/anti-corruption language (relevant given Hugel operates in 70+ countries — FCPA and local equivalents), conflict of interest disclosure process, gifts/hospitality limits relevant to HCP interactions in medical aesthetics, and a confidential reporting channel (whistleblower mechanism).

**Environmental health & safety (EHS)** — check for: applicable local safety regulation citations, incident reporting procedure and timeline, and training/certification requirements tied to specific roles.

**IT / security policies** — check for: data classification scheme, incident response procedure and timeline, and third-party/vendor access provisions.

## Red flags regardless of type

- No named owner or approval authority
- No review/update cadence (or one that's clearly lapsed — e.g., "next review: 2 years ago")
- Regulatory citations that reference outdated rule numbers or superseded guidance
- Requirements stated as recommendations ("should," "may") where the topic (safety, compliance, reporting) calls for a binding obligation
- No enforcement or consequences section at all
- Policy that conflicts with another named policy or standard practice it should align with
